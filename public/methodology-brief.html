How the tool works

A transparent look at how the tool turns national policy documents into clear, traceable insights on where commitments reinforce one another and where coordination could be stronger.

Why this tool exists

Across the new generation of Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), National Biodiversity Strategies and Action Plans (NBSAPs) and adaptation plans, ambition has risen, yet there is often limited clarity on how these commitments fit together and on the gaps between them. This tool provides data-based evidence to support that judgement. It is a decision-support system, not a decision-maker: it surfaces patterns for national experts to interpret, and the final reading always stays with policymakers.

How insights are created

Every result is produced by the same transparent sequence, organised in three phases. The AI reads only the policy text it is given; it never invents policy content.

Phase 1 · Extraction
Documents to targets
Read each page, set aside boilerplate, extract every target word-for-word with its source, merge duplicates, and verify each figure traces back to the original document.
→
Phase 2 · Analysis
Targets to coherence
Flag what is measurable and time-bound, classify each target against expert lenses, then compare every cross-document pair: one agent breaks a target into its parts, a second judges the alignment.
→
Phase 3 · Synthesis & Delivery
Pairs to patterns
Roll the judgements into coherency scores and coverage, surface where potential misalignment concentrates, and extend the same method to finance and implementation where the data exists.

Each pair is placed on a five-state scale. Four positive levels describe how strongly two targets align; a single review state, Potential misalignment, flags pairs for a human to examine rather than declaring a conflict.

The method in a little more detail

Classification (the lenses)

Targets are scored against expert reference frameworks: nature-based solutions, IPCC sectors, the GLOBE biodiversity taxonomy, and, where relevant, national adaptation goals. Categories are traced to published sources, never drafted by the AI, and are configurable per country. Each target keeps one primary category plus any others it clearly relates to, so the same evidence can be viewed through different lenses.

Comparison (two agents)

Separating the work into two steps (first decomposing a target into goal, action, area, actors and expected outcome, then assessing alignment from that structure) keeps judgements consistent across countries and policy frameworks, and grounded in content rather than shared keywords.

Why “potential misalignment”, not “conflict”

The negative side of the scale is deliberately cautious. A flag is a prompt for review, accompanied by the nature of the friction (competing goals, a shared resource, or delivery pressure), how manageable it looks, and how confident the reading is. The language is neutral and never frames a sector or ministry as at fault.

Synthesis

The tool produces short factual insights and visuals, not long AI-written narratives. Suggested next steps are framed as possibilities to explore (“could”, “may”), never instructions.

What the dashboard shows

  • Visuals plus short, factual insights (coverage, alignment patterns, and where coordination could be strengthened), never an automated report.
  • Switchable lenses, user-controlled, so the same analysis can be regrouped by theme, by sector (GLOBE, IPCC, or a country's own set), or by finance and implementation overlays, with no re-analysis.
  • A grounded chat that answers questions using only the analysed evidence.

Safeguards & trust

Human in the loop. The user confirms and edits the extracted targets before any analysis runs, and can rate any AI rationale with a thumbs and a note that feeds future improvement. Outputs are AI-assisted and require expert validation before use in policy processes.
Fully traceable. Every result links back to the source text it came from; nothing is asserted that cannot be checked.
No hallucinated content. The AI only classifies and compares the targets it is given.
Neutral language. Findings are framed as opportunities for stronger alignment, never as blame.
Data sovereignty. Government data runs on UNDP-controlled infrastructure and is not exposed to the open internet.
Frugal & Fair AI. A low-footprint design whose environmental cost is measured and reported openly.

The tool is being built as an open-source Digital Public Good, so that national governments can eventually run it on their own data and infrastructure.
