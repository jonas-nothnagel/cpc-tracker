How the analysis works

From policy documents to the coherence brief, one step at a time.

Phase 1 · ExtractionStep 01

Upload the policy documents

The method works for any set of national policy documents that set targets. The user provides two or more, each as a PDF, Word or text file and tagged with its type. Common examples are an NDC, an NBSAP, a NAP or an LDN target, plus sectoral strategies.

Phase 1 · ExtractionStep 02

Read every page, keep what matters

The tool reads each document page by page, including tables, and keeps track of where every passage came from. Forewords, annexes and boilerplate are set aside, so the careful work focuses on the passages that actually contain commitments.

chunk 1 · pp. 1–4Kept
chunk 2 · pp. 5–9Kept
chunk 3 · annexSet aside

Technical details

Page-aware parsers (pymupdf, python-docx) preserve page spans; text is chunked at ~30k characters with 2k overlap. A lightweight relevance filter then keeps or drops each chunk, and is deliberately conservative.

~30k chunks2k overlapkeep-on-doubt

The prompt

System
Sort one document chunk. Reply KEEP or DROP.
User
Does this passage contain concrete policy targets or commitments, or is it preface, boilerplate or annex? {chunk}
Rule
Defaults to KEEP whenever unsure, the cost is lost efficiency, never a missed target.

Phase 1 · ExtractionStep 03

Extract each target, word for word

From the relevant passages, each policy target is recorded together with the exact sentence it came from. The wording may be lightly tidied (a stray label dropped) but it is never reworded, summarised or embellished, and every target is labelled to show how.

Phase 1 · ExtractionStep 04

Consolidate, then verify nothing was invented

Because sections overlap, the same target can be picked up twice. Duplicates are merged into one entry (keeping all their source quotes), and then every figure, year and named framework is checked back against the original document.

Technical details

Consolidation merges near-duplicates (keeping every source); contradicting numbers stay as separate targets. Then validate_claim_grounding runs locally, a miss stamps a _provenanceFlag the dashboard surfaces as a review badge.

Phase 2 · AnalysisStep 05

Flag what is measurable and time-bound

For each target, the tool notes whether it contains a measurable outcome (a figure, a percentage, an area) and whether it is time-bound (a deadline). These are early indicators of how implementation-ready a commitment is, and the exact phrases are kept.

Worked example

The NBSAP target is time-bound ("by 2030"). The NDC water target names technologies and outcomes but no single headline figure, so it reads as qualitative here.

Technical details

Targets are evaluated in batches of 12 for isQuantitative / isTimeBound, returning the phrases. Administrative references ("Article 2") are excluded. Multilingual, no extra NLP dependencies.

Phase 2 · AnalysisStep 06

Classify each target by category

Every target is scored against published reference frameworks: the IPCC greenhouse gas inventory sectors, the GLOBE taxonomy, and the climate-resilience targets of the UAE Framework for Global Climate Resilience (the GGA). Each target keeps one primary category plus any others it clearly relates to. The lenses are interchangeable: on the dashboard the active lens can be switched with one click, and the same targets regroup under a different taxonomy while the classification itself does not re-run. Each country can also adjust the categories or add its own.

Technical details

A relevance score (0–1) for each (target × category); the top-scoring category becomes primary, and any above the relevance threshold are tagged relevant (dual-mode). Taxonomies: nature-based solutions, IPCC sectors, the GLOBE taxonomy with sub-categories, the seven GGA climate-resilience themes (UAE Framework for Global Climate Resilience, decision 2/CMA.5), and optional adaptation goals, all configurable per country. The most call-intensive step (n × c scores), so results are cached.

Phase 2 · AnalysisStep 07

Compare every target across documents

Coherence is about how different documents relate, so each target is paired with every target from a different document, never one against itself. Every pair is examined; nothing is filtered out in advance, so genuine cross-sector links are never missed.

Worked example

The NBSAP spatial-planning target is compared with 158 targets in the other seven documents; the NDC water target is one of them. Between the NBSAP and the NDC alone there are 720 target pairs.

Technical details

The cross-product of every different-document pairing, each pair counted once: for Mongolia's eight documents and 178 targets, 13,404 target pairs (NBSAP × NDC alone is 20 × 36 = 720). No pre-filter is applied, so a genuine cross-sector link is never screened out before the assessment looks at it.

cross-document onlyno pre-filterde-duplicated

The logic

Code (no LLM)
group targets by document → take the Cartesian product of each different-document pairing → de-duplicate by pair.

Phase 2 · AnalysisStep 08 · Agent 1

Break each target into its parts

Before two targets are compared, a first specialist agent, the Target Analyst, breaks each one into five plain components: its goal, the action proposed, the ecosystem, who is responsible, and the expected outcome. Comparing structured parts means alignment reflects policy substance, not word-matching.

Worked example · both targets, broken down

The water infrastructure in the NDC target's Action field (ponds, canals and pipelines) is what the comparison weighs against the NBSAP target's natural ecosystems.

Technical details

Agent 1 extracts five fields, strictly factual, no inference beyond the target text. Separating the breakdown (Agent 1) from the judgement (Agent 2) keeps assessments grounded and consistent across countries and frameworks.

Phase 2 · AnalysisStep 09 · Agent 2

Judge how well a pair aligns

A second agent, the Alignment Advisor, receives only the two breakdowns and places the pair on a five-level scale, or marks it as a "Potential misalignment", but only when it can name what pulls the two apart: a shared resource, the same place, or one undermining the other. A merely loose link stays "Partial". Every judgement carries a short rationale.

Worked example · the verdict

Potential misalignment

Competing for resources · manageable · medium confidence

Both targets support ecosystem protection and planning, but the NDC also names water accumulation facilities, irrigation ponds, canals and pasture irrigation, which can compete with the ecosystems the NBSAP target protects. Zoning, environmental-flow safeguards and biodiversity-sensitive siting would keep them aligned.

Technical details

Runs on gpt-5.4 (private Azure, temperature 0 for repeatable results). The pipeline's levels are none / low / medium / high / flagged; the dashboard shows low as "Partial" and flagged as "Potential misalignment". A potential misalignment also carries its type, manageability and confidence, so a reviewer sees why it was raised rather than a bare severity number.

Phase 3 · SynthesisStep 10

Score it, and map the whole picture

Finally, with no further AI, the individual judgements roll up into coherency scores between each pair of documents, plus a count of the potential misalignments to look at more closely. These feed the dashboard: the coherence wheel, alignment heatmaps and theme breakdowns.

NDC ↔ NBSAP coherency
19
potential misalignments (of 720 pairs)

Worked example · where it lands

The example pair is 1 of 19 potential misalignments between the NDC and NBSAP. The other 701 of 720 pairs sit on the positive scale (660 of them medium or high), so one barely moves the 70% score, but it is now visible and ready for review.

Technical details

Score = (3·high + 2·medium + 1·low) / (3·possible) × 100. For Mongolia's NDC and NBSAP that is 70% across 720 pairs, of which 19 are potential misalignments for review. All derived metrics are computed locally, zero model calls.

Phase 3 · SynthesisStep 11

Surface the recurring patterns

Across every pair, the tool shows how much potential misalignment there is and what kind it is. It is framed as where to look first: a prompt for review, never a verdict. Every result is AI-assisted and traceable to the source text, and the final interpretation rests with national experts.

How much shows potential misalignment

About 1 in 20 pairs shows potential misalignment (671 of 13,404). Most pairs align, so these are where to look first.

What kind it is

  • Delivery & coordination59%
  • Competing for resources32%
  • Conflicting goals9%

Worked example

In the NDC and NBSAP pairing, 19 of 720 pairs show potential misalignment, most of the delivery and coordination kind. The example pair is about competing for resources: coordinating water infrastructure with spatial planning could ease it.

Technical details

A synthesis layer reads the mechanism mix and surfaces recurring potential misalignment across document pairs, plus hedged pathway pointers, anchored to visible evidence, never prescriptive. In Mongolia's set about 5% of target pairs show potential misalignment, and about half of those involve just seven targets.

Phase 3 · DeliveryStep 12 · Level 2

Follow the finance

The same method compares each of a budget expenditure review's programme descriptions against the targets' text, showing where reviewed spending aligns with ambition, and where ambition has no aligned budget line.

Pasture management programmealigns →Pasture restoration target
Soil & fodder programmealigns →Sustainable agriculture
– no programme –no aligned line →Wetland restoration target

A BER covers only programmes in the uploaded review, a subset of national expenditure. The review identifies expenditure; every programme-to-target link is made by the Tracker's AI comparison of descriptions, not by the review.

Technical details

Level 2, finance. BER programmes become pseudo-targets and run through the same classify → decompose → align workflow; a funding view links programmes and reported actions by shared category. A target's "aligned spend" sums the programmes whose descriptions align with it (relative volume, never financing adequacy); a programme backs every target it aligns with, so per-target figures overlap while document totals count each programme once. Shipped for Mongolia and Panama.
