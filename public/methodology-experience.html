How the analysis works

From policy documents to the coherence brief, one step at a time.

Upload the policy documents

The method works with any set of national policy documents that set targets. The user uploads two or more, as PDF, Word or text files, and names each document’s type, for example an NDC, an NBSAP, a NAP, LDN targets or a sectoral strategy.

Technical details

Documents enter through POST /api/extract, with the document type recorded per file. The example on this page is Mongolia’s analysis: 8 documents and 178 targets.

PDFDOCXTXTdocument type per file

Read each document page by page

Each document is read page by page, tables included, and every passage keeps its page number. Forewords, annexes and boilerplate are set aside, so the extraction concentrates on the passages that set targets.

Technical details

Page-aware parsers (pymupdf, python-docx) keep the page spans. Text is split into chunks of about 30,000 characters with a 2,000-character overlap, and a light relevance filter keeps or drops each chunk, keeping it whenever unsure.

~30k chunks2k overlapkeep when unsure

The prompt

System
Sort one document chunk. Reply KEEP or DROP.
User
Does this passage contain concrete policy targets or commitments, or is it preface, boilerplate or annex? {chunk}
Rule
Defaults to KEEP when unsure: an extra read costs less than a missed target.

Extract each target, word for word

From the relevant passages, each policy target is recorded with the exact sentence it came from. The wording may be lightly tidied, for example a stray label dropped, but it is not reworded, summarised or extended, and each target is labelled to show how it was recorded.

Technical details

A strict verbatim contract: sourceText is copied character for character; the displayed text allows only deterministic clean-up (dropping a numeric label, rejoining broken lines). Every target keeps its sources and page numbers.

Merge duplicates and check the figures

Overlapping sections can pick up the same target twice. Duplicates are merged into one entry that keeps all their source quotes, and every figure, year and named framework is checked against the original document.

Technical details

Consolidation merges near-duplicates and keeps every source; targets with different numbers stay separate. A local check (validate_claim_grounding) then looks for every figure in the source quotes, and a miss marks the target for review (_provenanceFlag).

Confirm the targets

Before any comparison, the extracted targets go back to the user. Each is checked against its source and kept, corrected or removed, and targets the extraction missed can be added. The analysis starts only from a confirmed set.

Technical details

This is the review step of the upload wizard: targets can be kept, dropped, edited (text and page numbers) or added by hand. Rejected targets do not enter the pairing or rating steps.

human reviewkeep · edit · addgates the analysis

Note figures and deadlines

For each target, the tool notes whether it contains a measurable outcome (a figure, a percentage, an area) and whether it is time-bound (a year or a deadline), and keeps the exact phrases. Both are early indicators of how ready a commitment is for implementation.

The example pair

  • NBSAP · 1 Spatial planning

    A date: “By 2030”

  • NDC · Water resources 2

    Neither a figure nor a date

Technical details

Targets are checked in batches of 12 for isQuantitative and isTimeBound, and the phrases are returned. Administrative references (“Article 2”) are excluded. Works in any language, with no extra language-processing libraries.

Classify each target by policy area

Every target is scored against published reference frameworks, one per lens: Biodiversity (the GLOBE taxonomy), Climate mitigation (the IPCC greenhouse gas inventory sectors), Climate adaptation (the UAE Framework for Global Climate Resilience) and Human rights (UNDP guidance, draft under expert review). Each target keeps one primary area and any others it clearly relates to, so the brief can switch lenses without a new analysis. A country can add lenses of its own.

The example pair, under the Biodiversity lens

  • NBSAP · 1 Spatial planning

    Biodiversity planning and finance

  • NDC · Water resources 2

    Sustainable use

Technical details

A relevance score (0–1) for each (target × category); the top-scoring category becomes primary, and any above the relevance threshold are tagged relevant. Lenses: the GLOBE taxonomy with its subcategories, the IPCC greenhouse gas inventory sectors, the seven climate-resilience targets of the Global Goal on Adaptation (UAE Framework for Global Climate Resilience, decision 2/CMA.5) and nine human rights themes, plus a country’s own adaptation goals where it provides them. Nature-based solutions are paused. A country office can set its own lenses, as Sri Lanka did with its nine NDC 3.0 adaptation sectors in place of the Global Goal on Adaptation and a loss and damage lens. A lens with a single category has nothing to rank, so a target counts in it only above the relevance threshold. This is the most call-intensive step (n × c scores), so results are cached.

Pair every target across documents

Coherence is about how different documents relate, so each target is paired with every target of the other documents, and not with the targets of its own document. Every pair is rated, with no filter in advance, so links between distant sectors are read too.

Technical details

The cross-product of every pairing of different documents, each pair counted once: for Mongolia’s 8 documents and 178 targets, 13,404 target pairs (NBSAP × NDC alone is 20 × 36 = 720). No filter is applied before the rating.

different documents onlyno pre-filtereach pair once

The logic

Code (no LLM)
group targets by document → take the Cartesian product of each different-document pairing → de-duplicate by pair.

Break each target into its parts

Before two targets are compared, a first AI agent, the Target Analyst, breaks each one into five parts: its goal, the action proposed, the ecosystem or area, who is responsible, and the expected outcome. Comparing these parts grounds the rating in what each target sets out to do.

Technical details

Agent 1 extracts five fields, factual only, with no inference beyond the target text. Keeping the breakdown (Agent 1) apart from the rating (Agent 2) keeps ratings grounded and consistent across countries and frameworks.

Agent 1 · Target Analyst5 fieldsfactual only

Rate each target pair

A second AI agent, the Alignment Advisor, receives only the two breakdowns and rates the pair: strong, moderate or partial alignment, or no clear relationship. It rates a pair a potential misalignment only when it can name how the two interact: a shared resource, the same place, or one making the other harder to reach. A loose link stays partial. Every rating comes with a short explanation.

The pipeline stores none, low, medium, high or flagged; the brief shows them as no clear relationship, partial, moderate and strong alignment, and potential misalignment. A potential misalignment also records its type, manageability and confidence, so a reviewer sees why it was given.

Bring the ratings together

With no further AI, the ratings are counted: the share of target pairs that are aligned (strong or moderate alignment), partially aligned, or show potential misalignment, for the whole set, for each pair of documents and for each policy area. These shares are what the coherence brief shows.

The example pair

Between the NDC and the NBSAP, 92% of the 720 target pairs are aligned and 3% show potential misalignment. This pair is 1 of those 19.

Technical details

Counts are computed locally, with no model calls. Aligned counts strong and moderate alignment. For Mongolia: 66% of 13,404 target pairs are aligned, 29% partially aligned and 5% show potential misalignment.

Find recurring themes

Across all pairs, an AI synthesis groups the aligned and the potentially misaligned pairs into recurring themes, and each potential misalignment is counted by type. Both show where to look first; the final interpretation rests with national experts.

Of the 19 potential misalignments between the NDC and the NBSAP, 12 concern delivery and coordination and 7 compete for resources. This pair is 1 of the 7; the resource involved is land.

Technical details

A synthesis step reads the type mix and the pairs behind it and groups recurring themes across document pairs; a theme’s panel can add an AI-suggested starting point, anchored to the evidence shown. In Mongolia’s set, 5% of target pairs show potential misalignment, and 52% of those involve just 7 targets.

Rate the AI’s explanations

Every AI explanation in the brief can be rated with a thumbs up or down and an optional note. Ratings are anonymous and kept with the exact text they refer to, so reviewers can see where a reading helped and where it fell short, and the team can use them to improve later runs.

Technical details

Ratings are captured on target-pair explanations, document-pair summaries and theme summaries (POST /api/feedback) in an append-only ledger keyed to the exact AI text, so a rating stays interpretable after the pipeline is re-run.

thumbs + noteanonymousappend-only ledger

Compare budget programmes with the targets

The same steps compare the programmes of a Biodiversity Expenditure Review (BER) with the targets, showing which targets have a matching programme in the reviewed spending.

Technical details

Level 2, finance. BER programmes become pseudo-targets and run through the same classify, break-down and rate steps. A target’s matching spend sums the programmes whose descriptions align with it: a relative volume, not a measure of financing adequacy. A programme counts for every target it aligns with, so per-target figures overlap, while document totals count each programme once. A BER covers only the programmes it reviews, a subset of national expenditure; it identifies the spending, and every link to a target comes from the AI comparison of descriptions. Available for Mongolia and Panama.

Compare reported actions with the targets

The same steps compare actions reported in Biennial Transparency Reports (BTRs) and national reports with the targets: which targets the reported actions address, and where an action is potentially misaligned with a target.

Technical details

Level 3, implementation. Reported measures (BTR, and national reports such as the seventh national report to the Convention on Biological Diversity, NR7) become pseudo-targets through the same two-agent steps; an adapted prompt asks whether a measure directly implements, partially supports, or has no relationship to each target. Reported actions are the country’s own reporting, one view of implementation. Available for Mongolia and Panama; NR7 for Mongolia.
