# Draft response — Panama BER feedback (July 2026)

Status: **draft for the team (Lea/Michael) to adapt and send** to the BIOFIN Panama technical focal point. Point-by-point against `panama-financing-2026-07.txt`. Written to be sendable in plain language; trim as needed.

Dear [focal point],

Thank you for the careful review of the financial module — it identified exactly the places where the interface claimed more than the analysis supports, and we have made changes in response to each point.

**1. Analytical perspective of the module.** You are right that expenditure originates in budget allocations and is not attached to any single policy document. The module keeps its document-organised layout, but the slide now says explicitly that each policy document is one analytical lens over the same reviewed spending, and that amounts therefore repeat across documents and cannot be summed. A new "How these figures are produced" note on the slide explains the full chain from budget allocation → BER → Tracker analysis.

**2. Interpretation of expenditure alignment.** The interface now states directly — in a provenance badge on the grid and in the methodology note — that the BER identifies and quantifies biodiversity expenditure but does not assign it to policy targets, and that every programme-to-target link shown is generated by the Tracker's AI, which compares each funding line's description with each target's text. All wording that could read as money flowing to a target has been replaced with alignment-between-descriptions language.

**3. Terminology.** We adopted your proposal. "Well-funded", "Funded" and "Under-funded" are gone; the tiers are now "High aligned spend", "Medium aligned spend", "Low aligned spend" and "No aligned spend", with captions explaining that they describe relative levels of aligned expenditure within the reviewed set — never financing adequacy or sufficiency. "Aligned spend" stays, as you suggested.

**4. Presentation of expenditure records.** The descriptive fields Panama prepared were already ingested but too hidden. Each programme row now shows its institution and the descriptive text from the complementary dataset directly in the list, so records with identical programme names (e.g. the two "Transferencias Varias" lines) are distinguishable at a glance. The full description, code, and per-year spend remain one click deeper.

**5. Thematic expenditure categories.** We have added an interim "Reviewed spend by GLOBE category" breakdown to the financing view, using the BIOFIN global taxonomy (which closely mirrors your proposed eight categories). It is clearly labeled as assigned by the Tracker's AI from programme descriptions — it is not BIOFIN Panama's classification. When your thematic classification of the BER is ready, we would be glad to ingest it as the authoritative layer and replace or sit it alongside the AI-assigned view.

**6. Methodological transparency.** Your four questions are now answered inside the interface, in the "How these figures are produced" note:
- *What is an aligned programme?* One whose description the AI judged closely or moderately aligned with the target's text, with executed spend in the review period.
- *Multiple targets?* A programme appears under every target it aligns with, carrying its full spend each time — a measure of association, not allocation.
- *How to read per-target values?* As a relative signal for comparing targets, not as evidence of financing sufficiency.
- *Double counting?* Per-target figures intentionally overlap; document and review totals now count each programme once, and the interface says so. (This also corrects the previous document totals, which summed the overlapping figures — the displayed totals are now lower, which is the fix.)

**Considered but deferred, for transparency:** apportioning a programme's spend across the targets it aligns with (a methodological change we would not make without BIOFIN validation), and an expenditure-first navigation (programme → targets). Both remain open if useful in future rounds.

Thank you again — this kind of review is what makes the module credible.
